## Verdict

**Both narrow questions survive the inspected literature; neither has unconditional scientific clearance. Proceed to measurement validation, with a baseline/positioning supplement before confirmatory freeze.** New close neighbors weaken broad novelty claims. P1 has a clearer incremental intervention question; P2 must demonstrate an incremental *verification-status effect* beyond ordinary evidence-scope failures. Existing code does not count in favor of either decision.

The strict requirement for a baseline that reproduces a prior known setting is only partly met. Current no-tool/no-verification arms are experimental baselines, not direct replications of published settings. Do not describe them as replications. Either specify a small, separately labeled reproduction/bridge module or explicitly narrow the contribution to a within-task contrast, explaining why that comparison establishes new knowledge without reproducing the full original paradigm.

## P1: closest experimental comparisons

| Study, access depth | What ran and what it found | What P1 adds / does not add |
|---|---|---|
| [Christian & Mazor, Self-Blinding](https://matanmazor.github.io/files/papers/christian2026blinding.pdf); fresh PDF abstract and [author repository README](https://github.com/self-model/SelfBlindingLLMs/blob/main/sycophancy/README.md) inspected; detailed PDF re-fetch timed out, so detailed findings also rely on the earlier recorded full-text review | Ambiguous disputes with user-side/order counterbalancing; separate proactive self-simulation-tool use and post-tool judgment measures. | [Correction or Manipulation?](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36453), §§3–4 and Study 2 methods inspected fresh | Correct/incorrect challenges, graded pressure and neutral reconsideration; citations worsen discrimination in several models. Account-support study supplies valid/invalid verification state in a system message, then measures action policy compliance; sending a code is available but supplied status determines permission. | Pressure × user correctness and verification-related behavior already studied. P1 adds randomized optional retrieval versus supplied identical records for factual correctness. Pressure alone is not a gap. |
| **New closer operational neighbor:** [AgentDecept](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.39377), §§3,5, Appendix C.5–6 inspected | 477 pressure variants from 257 tasks, paired with pressure-free counterparts. User-expectation tasks contain checkable premises and accessible API records. Reports compare claims with observations and execution; pressure timing also studied. | Real tool use under pressure with objective evidence already exists. No optional-versus-supplied evidence intervention was found. P1 can isolate a workflow intervention the benchmark does not identify. |
| **New native-tool neighbor:** [Is Sycophancy a Form of In-Context Updating?](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28325), behavioral methods and Appendices A/B inspected | Compares support sources, user versus source conflicts and audience access; Appendix A has forced native lookup_answer calls, and B2 compares user claim, user-reported tool output and native tool result containing the same incorrect answer. Results support source-sensitive updating beyond approval seeking. | Native provenance is not a novel treatment. Calls are forced; no choice-to-check versus supplied contrast found. Warns against interpreting observed pressure susceptibility as a particular internal motivation. |

**Knowledge sentence.** Existing work shows pressure-sensitive factual updating and limited voluntary use of debiasing tools. It does not, in these inspected comparisons, establish whether pressure changes retrieval of objectively decisive records, and whether guaranteed delivery attenuates the pressure penalty. Randomizing pressure × delivery regime × user correctness estimates that interaction, useful for deciding whether guaranteed delivery helps in the tested workflow.

**Smallest convincing design.** Neutral reconsideration versus one validated pressure treatment × optional lookup versus supplied record × correct/wrong user assertion; matched cached initial answer; all randomized cases analyzed intention-to-treat. Keep the no-tool arm as a conceptual pressure baseline, not a direct replication of Christian & Mazor. More pressure types are robustness/generalization, not necessary for the core identification.

**Baseline supplement.** A compact published-setting replication could use Christian & Mazor's released ambiguous-dispute prompts, tool and post-tool readouts before moving to objective records, or reproduce Correction or Manipulation's published factual challenge subset. This is additional planned work, not implemented or mandatory proof that the within-task causal contrast is valid. If the strict user gate requires a literal prior-setting baseline, it remains open until one is specified. The bridge must not silently combine ambiguous normative truth with objective correctness in one pooled metric.

**Outcomes.** Reduced retrieval plus attenuation under supplied evidence supports guaranteed delivery as a remedy in this task; persistent supplied-arm errors show that delivery alone is insufficient; increased retrieval challenges the predicted harmful direction; precise null with nonfloor retrieval and working manipulation establishes a boundary. Floor/ceiling, failed pressure manipulation or wide intervals teach little about the hypothesis and require measurement redesign, not a success claim.

**Claim limit / main alternative.** Workflow effect, not proof of a cognitive mediator. Optional agents act before receiving evidence; injection may change timing/attention even with identical message formatting. Do not call the supplied condition a complete isolation of evidence 'use' from all procedural differences.

## P2: closest experimental comparisons

| Study, access depth | Actual experiment/finding | Remaining contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **New close past submission:** [False Containment](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/false-containment-measuring-the-evidence-required-to-verify-autonomous-ai-incident-resolution-n5i5), linked full nine-page report methods/results/limitations inspected; public repository landing page inspected, not executed | Four synthetic persistence families, paired resolved/unresolved states, cumulative evidence packets. Animal welfare alone is not the difference. |
| [Silence Is Endorsement](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.20211), methods and Appendix J freshly checked; remainder in prior follow-up | Same authorization proposition across absent/unverified/lost-status/verified states; status loss increases approval. Appendix J passes identical status content through prose, JSON and typed formats; preservation depends on summarizer. | Cross-claim P→Q transfer with true preserved P-verification differs from same-claim status loss. C′−C is a robustness/application comparison, not standalone format novelty. |
| [Who Verifies the Tools?](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32110), methods/results and failure examples inspected | Fixed computed results with incomplete versus repaired disclosure about scope, caps, estimates or identifier spaces; omission changes overclaims and verification judgments. Repaired descriptions add facts and instructions. | Scope clarification as a mitigation is already known. Primary contribution must rest on B−A/B−D1, not just C−B. |
| [Authority Bias](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.37616), methods/abstract inspected | Wrong answers attributed to verified sources override baseline-correct responses; source and user cues differ under matched behavioral and activation interventions. | Q itself is falsely endorsed there. P2 uses true verification of distinct P, separating claim-level attribution from rational confidence changes. Topic-sensitive authority can still mediate the result. |
| [Does the word verified steer what action an AI model favors?](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/projects/does-the-word-verified-steer-what-action-an-ai-model-favors-5taz), public project page inspected; earlier recorded review includes its fixed-fact label design | Prior Apart project swaps Verified/Preliminary labels while preserving scenario facts. | Fixed-fact label manipulation alone is not new. Needs distinct Q attribution, actual bounded audit semantics and unrelated-check specificity. |

**Knowledge sentence.** Prior work establishes scope errors, status-cue effects and narrow checks being mistaken for overall completion. P2 asks whether adding *truthful verification status* to a fixed practice fact causes unsupported attribution that a separate animal-based outcome was verified, and whether that increment is specific to the checked content. B−A and B−D1, with an attribution ground truth independent of belief, test this increment.

**Minimum comparison.** A, B, D1 plus genuine positive and negative outcome evidence E, with one narrowly defined outcome metric. C tests scope disclosure; C′ format is secondary. Facility/time/population/outcome and independent auditor must be matched. Correctly inferring Q is more likely must never count as incorrect verification attribution.

**Baseline supplement.** A is a useful ordinary evidence-sufficiency baseline; it does not reproduce False Containment or Silence. A compact bridge should reproduce a known same-claim verification-status manipulation, then add a distinct-claim target with matched content and separate attribution scoring. An explicit claim-ID binding rule is a useful deterministic benchmark, and a force-aware support rubric is a stronger language-model comparison than an unassisted generic answer. Choose and preregister one bridge rather than inflating the main factorial indiscriminately. The current dossier arms do not by themselves satisfy a literal published-setting replication gate.

**Results.** B>A and B>D1 supports content-specific cross-claim verification uplift; it does not identify whether authority or inference mediates it. B and D1 equivalently above A supports nonspecific status influence, provided equivalence is established rather than inferred from nonsignificance. High A with no incremental B effect is ordinary scope/entailment failure, closely covered already; describe it as a replication/boundary, not discovery of spillover. A precise null with working E controls still informs robustness. C reducing attribution does not demonstrate no cost to warranted trust unless clarification is also tested when Q is genuinely established.

**Strongest alternative.** Practice information is legitimately evidence about outcomes. This is handled only by sharply separating audit-attribution from probability. Animal-welfare labels also vary in real scope; some audit actual animal outcomes. Fictional narrow audits cannot establish that certification in general creates false assurance. Domain-reviewed real cases and validated attribution language are still required for application claims.
